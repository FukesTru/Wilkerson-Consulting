Manufacturing Cost Accounting

Manufacturing cost accounting for Reno area manufacturers

Wilkerson Consulting in Reno, Nevada provides manufacturing cost accounting.

Ray Wilkerson, CPA, works out what each product or job really costs to make, from materials and labor to shop overhead. He ties those costs to your inventory and your profit and loss statement, so you can price and quote from real numbers.

Warning signs

Do you know what each product really costs to make?

If any of these are true, your product costs are probably a guess.

  • Sales grow but profit does notSome products or customers may be losing money and hiding inside the total.
  • Quotes start from last year’s priceMaterial and labor costs have moved, and the quote has not.
  • The inventory count never matches the booksEach year-end brings a write-down nobody can explain.
  • Overhead is one big numberRent, power, repairs and supervision are never spread across what you make.
  • Labor is not tracked by job or productSo nobody knows which work takes longer than planned.

The basics

What goes into the cost of a product?

Every manufactured product carries 3 kinds of cost. Getting the third one right is where most shops struggle.

Direct materials

The raw materials and parts that go into the product, including freight in and normal scrap.

Direct labor

The pay and payroll costs of the people who make the product, tracked by job or by product.

Manufacturing overhead

Every other production cost, such as shop rent, utilities, equipment repairs and supervisors’ pay.

Overhead is spread across products with an overhead rate, also called a burden rate. That is a cost per machine hour, per labor hour or per unit, based on how your shop really works.

Choosing a method

Job costing or process costing, which fits your shop?

The right method depends on how you make things. Some shops use a mix of both.

Job costing

For custom and made-to-order work

Each order or job collects its own materials, labor and overhead. It fits machine shops, fabricators, cabinet makers and anyone who builds to a customer’s drawing.

Process costing

For steady runs of the same product

Costs are gathered by production step or department and divided over the units made. It fits food and beverage producers and other continuous production.

The rules

Which accounting and tax rules touch inventory costs?

This is general information. Confirm what applies to your business with your own CPA or tax preparer.

On your financial statements

US accounting standards require inventory to include the costs of making it, including a fair share of production overhead. Inventory that is damaged, obsolete or worth less than it cost may need to be written down.

On your tax return

Federal tax rules, often called the uniform capitalization rules, decide which costs must stay in inventory until the product is sold. Smaller businesses may be exempt. Ray prepares the cost detail your tax preparer needs to apply them.

In Nevada

Nevada has no state personal or corporate income tax. Manufacturers still deal with Nevada business taxes, such as the Modified Business Tax on wages, so clean payroll and cost records matter here too.

What is a standard cost?

A standard cost is the planned cost to make one unit of a product, set ahead of time from expected materials, labor and overhead. Comparing it with actual cost each month shows where production is off plan.

What is a variance?

A variance is the difference between the standard cost and the actual cost. A materials price variance means you paid more or less than planned, and a usage variance means you used more or less than planned.

How often should we count inventory?

At least once a year for your financial statements. Many manufacturers also count a few items every week or month, called cycle counting, so errors are found and fixed while they are small.

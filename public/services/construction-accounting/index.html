What should you do first if your WIP schedule does not add up?

Before you send a WIP schedule to a bank or a surety, check these 5 things. They cause most of the problems Ray sees.

  1. Get fresh estimates to complete

    Ask each project manager what it will really cost to finish each job. An old estimate is the biggest single cause of a wrong WIP.

  2. Put approved change orders in the contract

    A change order is a signed change to the job’s scope and price. If the cost is in the books but the extra price is not, the job looks worse than it is.

  3. Check where costs are coded

    Labor or materials charged to the wrong job make one job look great and another look terrible.

  4. Match billings to your pay applications

    What the WIP shows as billed should match what you actually invoiced, including retainage.

  5. Never plug a number to make it balance

    A forced number hides the problem until year end, when the outside CPA finds it.

How it works

What is a WIP schedule, and how does percentage of completion work?

A work in progress (WIP) schedule is a report that lists every open job with its contract amount, estimated total cost, cost to date, percent complete, revenue earned and amount billed. Banks and sureties read it to judge whether your jobs are making the money you expected. A surety is the company that writes your bid, performance and payment bonds.

Percentage of completion is a way of counting revenue as a job progresses instead of when it is finished. Most contractors measure progress by cost. A job that has used half its estimated cost is treated as half complete, so half the contract has been earned.

The difference between what you have earned and what you have billed is your over or under billing. Overbilled means you have billed ahead of the work, which is a liability. Underbilled means the work is ahead of your billing, which needs a close look.

The rules

Which rules shape construction accounting in Nevada and California?

This is general information. Your own CPA, tax preparer or attorney should confirm what applies to your business.

Revenue on your financial statements

Under US accounting standards, revenue from contracts with customers is covered by a standard known as ASC 606. Many construction contracts meet its test for counting revenue over time as the work is done, and the cost to cost method is a common way to measure that progress.

Revenue on your tax return

Tax rules for long-term contracts are separate from the accounting rules. Smaller contractors may qualify for other methods on their tax return. Ray prepares the schedules your tax preparer needs to make that call.

Contractor licensing

In Nevada, contractors are licensed by the Nevada State Contractors Board, which sets a monetary limit on each license. In Truckee and on the California side of Lake Tahoe, contractors are licensed by the Contractors State License Board. Accurate financial records help when you apply or ask for a change.

Questions and answers

Questions contractors ask about WIP and job costs

How often should a contractor update the WIP schedule?

Monthly is the common standard, because cost and billing change every month. Many banks and sureties ask for it at least every quarter and at year end. A monthly WIP schedule also catches a problem job while there is still time to act.

What is profit fade?

Profit fade is when the expected profit on a job shrinks as the job goes on. It usually means the estimate to complete was too low. A monthly WIP schedule with honest estimates from your project managers shows fade early.

What is retainage?

Retainage is the part of each progress payment the owner or general contractor holds back until the job is finished, often 5% or 10%. It is money you have earned but not yet collected, so it needs its own tracking.

Do small contractors need percentage of completion?

If a bank, a surety or an outside CPA relies on your financial statements, they will usually expect it. For taxes, smaller contractors may be able to use a different method. Ask your tax preparer which applies to you.
